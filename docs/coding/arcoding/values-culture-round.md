## Values / culture round

<div class="adm warn"><div class="adm-title">⚠️ Highest failure rate</div>
<p>Per Anthropic recruiters, this is where most candidates fail. Interviewers probe 3–4 follow-ups deep and want honest reasoning under ambiguity, not hero stories. Prepare real stories; say the uncomfortable part first.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h4 style="margin-top:0">"Tell me about a time you did something that conflicted with your own values."</h4>
<p>Pick a genuine conflict with stakes. Structure: situation → what you actually felt and why → what you did → what it cost → what you'd do differently, concretely. Follow-ups test whether the reflection is real ("why didn't you escalate?", "would you do the same today?"). A polished story with no residue of discomfort reads as evasion.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h4 style="margin-top:0">"How would you handle being assigned to a project you believed was unsafe?"</h4>
<p>Show a process, not a posture: verify your understanding (steelman the other side); raise the concern specifically, in writing, with the responsible people; propose alternatives; escalate through legitimate channels; be honest about your personal line. Both "I'd trust leadership" and "I'd immediately refuse/leak" fail.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h4 style="margin-top:0">"Tell me about a time you pushed back on a decision and lost."</h4>
<p>Disagree-and-commit maturity: argued with evidence, lost legitimately, committed genuinely (no sandbagging), tracked the outcome. Strong endings are honest either way: "they were right — here's what I underweighted" or "it failed as predicted — here's how I raised it again without I-told-you-so." A reliability-vs-velocity dispute is on-theme for SRE.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h4 style="margin-top:0">"Why Anthropic, and what's your take on AI risk?"</h4>
<p>Generic "AI is powerful and could be dangerous" fails. Connect your craft to the mission: reliability engineering is a safety discipline — predictable, observable, degradable systems are the substrate of safe deployment; capacity and incident discipline are how a lab keeps RSP-style promises under pressure. Hold genuine uncertainty: name an unsettled question rather than reciting positions.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h4 style="margin-top:0">"Describe a time you changed your mind about something you felt strongly about."</h4>
<p>Epistemic humility. Pick a real reversal on real evidence ("I dismissed formal SLOs as bureaucracy until an error budget ended a pager war"). Name the evidence that moved you and what it changed about how you form opinions now.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h4 style="margin-top:0">"Tell me about your worst incident. What was your role?"</h4>
<p>Show the full arc: detection (monitoring or customer?), diagnosis including wrong turns (wrong turns are credibility), mitigation-before-root-cause sequencing, communication cadence, and the systemic fix (the failure <em>class</em> you eliminated). Bring numbers: time to detect, time to mitigate, blast radius. Expect the 4-deep drill: "why the retry storm?" → "why no retry budget?" → "what changed in review so the next service has one?"</p></div>
